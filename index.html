08 — Before you call the insurer

Questions we get
every single week

Mostly about insurance, because the industry has spent decades making a simple process feel complicated.

My insurer told me to use their approved shop. Do I have to?

No. You have the right under your policy to choose your own repairer — an insurer can recommend a network shop, but steering you away from the one you picked isn't something they're entitled to do. We work directly with seven insurers and bill them the same way their own network shops do.

Will you match or waive my excess?

No, and be careful with any shop that offers to. Waiving an excess is insurance fraud, and it gets paid for somewhere — usually in aftermarket parts, a skipped calibration, or a spot repair where a blend was needed. Your excess is what it says on your policy schedule.

How long will it actually take?

Our average is 6.4 days from drop-off to delivery. A bumper cover and a blend is two to three days. Structural work waiting on an imported OEM part can run three to four weeks — most of that is the part clearing customs, not the labour. You'll get a delivery date at blueprint, once we know what's actually under there, and a text if it moves.

Do you use aftermarket or non-genuine parts?

We quote OEM by default. If your policy schedule specifies aftermarket or "non-genuine" parts, we'll tell you exactly which lines are affected and what the OEM difference costs, and you can pay the gap if you want to. We won't fit an aftermarket structural part or a non-OEM airbag component at any price.

Do I get a courtesy car?

We keep four courtesy cars on the lot, free, first come. If they're out and your policy includes a hire car benefit, we'll set it up with Europcar before you arrive so you're not standing at the counter after dropping the car off.

Why does the paint need to be blended into the next panel?

Because factory colour drifts and a hard edge shows. Spraying only the repaired panel leaves a line you'll see every sunny day for the rest of the car's life. Blending fades the new colour into the adjacent panel under a common coat of clear, so there's no edge to find. It costs a couple of labour hours and it's the difference between a repair and a visible repair.
